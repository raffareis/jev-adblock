Central bank holds rates as markets rally into the close

By Ana Ribeiro · Updated 5 October 2026

Policymakers left the benchmark rate unchanged on Wednesday, citing easing inflation and a labour market that has cooled without cracking. Stocks extended gains after the announcement, with financials and technology leading the advance.

The decision was widely expected. What moved markets was the statement's softer tone on future tightening, which traders read as an opening for cuts early next year. Two-year yields fell to their lowest level since spring.

"The balance of risks has shifted," the bank said, adding that it would watch wage growth and services prices closely over the coming months. Analysts noted that the committee vote was unanimous for the third meeting in a row.

Currency markets were calmer. The dollar slipped against a basket of major peers, while emerging-market currencies posted modest gains as investors returned to higher-yielding assets.

Commodities were mixed. Crude oil edged lower on signs of rising inventories, while gold held near a record as real yields declined.

Economists expect the next data releases on retail sales and consumer prices to set the tone for the December meeting, when the bank will also publish updated projections.

Corporate earnings season begins next week, with the largest banks reporting first. Margins are expected to narrow as deposit costs rise, though trading revenue may offset part of the pressure.

Bond investors have piled into longer maturities in recent weeks, betting that the tightening cycle is over. Fund flows into government debt reached their highest level of the year in September.

Small businesses remain cautious. A survey published on Tuesday showed hiring plans at their weakest in three years, even as sales expectations improved slightly.

Housing data due on Friday will offer another read on how higher borrowing costs are filtering through the economy.
